## Named

Before you've evaluated the next setup, you've already seen the balance. A green number makes the
next entry feel safer than it is. A red one makes the next entry feel like something to fix in one
trade. The figure carries no decision content — and it decides the decision anyway.

## What actually fixes it

The number that trains this reaction has to leave the screen. What replaces it needs to describe
the trade without describing your mood: a share of the account, not an amount you could spend.

## How the platform closes it

<!-- E-C121 -->
Money values are removed from the trading surface — the one exception is account size at the
point you connect it, which the size calculation needs anyway. What you see instead is a
percentage of account at risk and the outcome measured in R, never a currency figure.

## What it competes against

Checking the account balance or the day's payout figure in the broker terminal or the firm's
dashboard — accurate, and also the exact number that trains overconfidence after a win and
oversized entries after a loss, independent of what the setup in front of you actually looks like.
