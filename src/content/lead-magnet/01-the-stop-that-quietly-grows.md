## Named

You place a trade with a defined stop. Price moves against you. The stop is still just a line on
a chart in a terminal that lets you move it — and the moment it hurts most is exactly the moment
you're most tempted to. What started as a fixed, known risk becomes an open one, at the worst
possible time to discover that.

## What actually fixes it

Risk has to be fixed before the trade starts, and structurally impossible to redefine once it's
live. Not a rule you promise yourself in a calm moment — a fact that holds in an uncalm one.

## How the platform closes it

<!-- E-C181 -->
A stop is set at the moment the order goes in, and it cannot be widened afterward — not as a
setting, not as an exception. The same fixed stop that defines the risk is also what the trade is
measured against, so "the trade can't lose more than this" and "the trade is shown in R, not in
an open-ended number" are one mechanism, not two separate features that could drift apart.

## What it competes against

Opening the same terminal that placed the trade and dragging the stop-loss line further away once
price turns — the standard exit every retail platform leaves open by default, and the one move
that turns a defined loss into an undefined one.
